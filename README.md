# Bubroo

Bubroo is an AI-powered professional networking and career development platform prototype designed around one core problem: access to opportunity is still shaped too much by opaque networks, weak signaling, and fragmented information.

The project was originally developed for the Columbia 2024 startup competition. It was built as a platform concept exploring how skill visibility, mentorship, and more structured matching could help reduce information gaps and improve access to career opportunities.

## What Bubroo is trying to solve

Bubroo was built around several recurring issues in the job market:

- information gaps and networking inequality
- poor visibility of real skills beyond CVs and titles
- high stress and poor user experience in existing job platforms
- weak support for career changers and cross-disciplinary candidates
- limited access to mentorship and practical guidance

The idea was to approach these not just as recruiting problems, but as product and system design problems.

![diagram](img/Screenshot%202026-05-29%20165615.jpg)

## Core product ideas

Bubroo combines several layers:

- **Skill-centric transparency**  
  User profiles are designed to show applied skills more clearly, rather than relying only on resumes and credentials.

- **AI-powered personalization**  
  The platform explores AI-assisted career recommendations, matching, and skill-gap guidance.

- **Mentorship as part of the platform**  
  Users with experience can share practical guidance, creating a more accessible and less elite version of career coaching.

- **A more intuitive and less anxiety-driven interface**  
  The product direction aimed to feel closer to a modern, supportive digital platform than to a traditional job board.

## Why this project matters to US

What interested me in Bubroo was not just “better networking,” but the deeper structure underneath it. Too much of career access still depends on visibility, timing, and social proximity rather than on legible capability. Bubroo was an attempt to think about that gap as a platform problem.
